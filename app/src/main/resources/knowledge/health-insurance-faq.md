# Australian private health insurance — what the agent may say

Everything the agent states as fact must come from this file. Entries are short because
each answer has to fit an SMS.

**This content is illustrative and has not been reviewed by anyone who owns the product
facts. Figures marked (check) change with the financial year and must be confirmed before
this answers a real person.**

## The two kinds of cover

- **Hospital cover** pays for treatment as a private patient in hospital. It is sold in
  four tiers: Basic, Bronze, Silver, Gold. Each tier has a minimum list of clinical
  categories it must cover, set by the government, so tiers are comparable across funds.
- **Extras (general treatment)** covers things Medicare does not: dental, optical,
  physio, and similar. It is not tiered, so extras policies differ a lot between funds.
- They can be bought separately or as a combined policy.

## Waiting periods

Maximum waiting periods are set by law. A fund may be shorter, never longer.

- Pre-existing conditions: **12 months**
- Pregnancy and birth: **12 months**
- Palliative care, rehabilitation, psychiatric care: **2 months**, even if pre-existing
- Everything else on hospital cover: **2 months**
- Extras: set by the fund, commonly 2 months for general dental and 12 months for major
  dental or optical

## Switching funds

- Waiting periods already served on an **equivalent level of cover** carry over. You do
  not serve them again.
- If the new policy covers something the old one did not, or at a higher tier, the
  waiting period applies to that new part only.
- There is no fee to switch, and no need to wait for a renewal date.
- The old fund issues a clearance certificate; the new fund usually requests it.

## Lifetime Health Cover (LHC) loading

- If you take out hospital cover after **1 July following your 31st birthday**, a loading
  of **2% per year** is added to your premium, to a maximum of **70%**.
- The loading is removed after **10 continuous years** of holding hospital cover.
- It applies to hospital cover only, not extras.

## Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS)

- Applies to people **without hospital cover** whose income is above the threshold, in
  addition to the ordinary Medicare levy.
- Threshold is roughly **$97,000 for singles and $194,000 for families** (check), rising
  with dependants.
- The surcharge is **1% to 1.5%** of income depending on the tier (check).
- Extras cover does not exempt you. Only hospital cover does.

## The government rebate

- A means-tested rebate reduces the premium, taken either as a lower premium or as a tax
  offset.
- The percentage falls as income rises and is higher for people **65 and over** (check).
- Thresholds are adjusted on 1 April each year (check).

## Other common questions

- **Premiums change on 1 April** each year, when funds apply an approved increase.
- **Ambulance cover** varies by state: it is covered by the state in Queensland and
  Tasmania, and usually needs cover or a subscription elsewhere.
- **Gap** is what you pay when a doctor charges more than the fund and Medicare pay
  between them. Funds have gap-cover arrangements with some doctors and not others.
- **Excess** is the amount you agree to pay towards a hospital admission in exchange for
  a lower premium.
- **Overseas visitors** need a different product (OVHC) and cannot buy resident cover.
