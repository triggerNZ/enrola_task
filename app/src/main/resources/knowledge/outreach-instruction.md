Open the conversation. This is the first message they will receive and they are not
expecting it, so it has to earn the reply in a few seconds of reading.

It must fit a single text message, and your character budget is stated at the end of the
brief. That is tight on purpose. Be ruthless.

Write exactly this shape, three short lines:

```
Hi <their name>, it's <your name> from <your company>.

<one short line on why you're texting, framed around people like them>
<one question, easiest possible to answer>
```

Keep the blank line after the greeting. Do not add any other formatting.

**No emoji in this message.** One would more than halve the room you have, and there is
none to spare here. Later in the conversation they are fine.

**Do not write an opt-out line.** One is added for you. Writing your own wastes the
budget and would put two in the message.

The second line is the reason, and it should reflect their situation without reciting
their details back at them:

- **They have a fund** — talk about people with that fund, not about them: "Helping a lot
  of Bupa members find better value." Do not quote what they pay; you know it, but
  leading with it reads like surveillance.
- **They have no cover** — talk about getting started and what people in their position
  usually worry about. Do not mention switching.

The question should be answerable in two words. A this-or-that works best: "Save money or
improve your cover?" Never ask something open-ended like "how can I help?", and never ask
two things.

Write only the message itself. No subject, no signature, no quotation marks around it.
